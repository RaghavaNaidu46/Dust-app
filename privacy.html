Privacy Policy

Last updated 22 November 2026

Dust collects nothing.

That is the whole policy. Here is what it means concretely.

No network

Dust makes no network requests. There is no server, no analytics, no crash reporting, no update check, no account and no licence check — nothing for it to send anything to. You can confirm this with Little Snitch, LuLu or nettop and watch it make no connections at all.

What Dust reads

To tell you how much space you can reclaim, Dust reads file sizes and dates across your disk: caches, logs, build output, and any folders you add yourself in Settings. It reads metadata only — how large a file is and when it was last touched. It does not open files or read their contents.

This is why Dust asks for Full Disk Access. macOS protects the folders where app caches live, and without that permission Dust reports a number that is too low. The permission is used for sizing and for removing what you choose, nothing else. Dust works without it and says plainly when the totals are incomplete.

What Dust writes

Two files, both inside ~/Library/Application Support/Dust/:

  • snapshot.json — the result of your last scan, so the panel opens instantly instead of re-walking your disk.
  • settings.json — your preferences: scan roots, protected paths, thresholds.

Both stay on your Mac. Deleting that folder resets Dust completely.

What Dust deletes

Only what you click. Scanning never writes anything. Your documents, Desktop, Pictures, Movies, Music, iCloud Drive, Keychains, Mail, Messages and iOS device backups are excluded before Dust even builds its list of candidates, so they cannot be selected by accident.

Anything recoverable goes to the Trash. Only regenerable caches are deleted outright, and anything that cannot be undone is called out before it happens.

Children

Dust is a disk utility with no accounts, no content and no communication features. It collects no data from anyone, of any age.

Changes

If this policy ever changes, the revision date above changes with it. Since Dust has no network access, a policy change cannot alter what a copy already installed on your Mac does.
